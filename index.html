🔭 SPHEREx Sky Viewer — User Guide

What is SPHEREx?

NASA's SPHEREx (Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization and Ices Explorer) is mapping the entire sky every six months in 102 bands of near-infrared light (0.75–5.0 µm). Launched March 2025, it has already imaged over a billion objects.

How to Use This Tool

  1. Enter coordinates — Type RA/Dec in decimal degrees or sexagesimal (e.g., 17h57m48.5s), or enter an object name and click "Resolve" to look it up via SIMBAD.
  2. Search — Click "Search Sky" to query the IRSA archive for SPHEREx images at those coordinates.
  3. Blink — Use the playback controls to animate between different observation epochs. Moving objects like asteroids and nearby stars will appear to jump between frames!
  4. Analyze — Use Diff mode to subtract images, Stack mode to combine them, and extract spectra from the 102 infrared bands.

Discovering Moving Objects

Many astronomical discoveries have been made by volunteers using the "blink" technique — comparing images taken at different times. When objects move between observations:

  • Asteroids & comets shift due to their solar orbits
  • Nearby stars show "proper motion" across the sky
  • Brown dwarfs appear as dim, moving infrared sources
  • Variable stars change brightness over time
